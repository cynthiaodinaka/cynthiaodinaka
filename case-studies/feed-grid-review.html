Design Review — 5 Grids

Reading the Feed

Five Instagram grid layouts, reviewed as full-page systems rather than nine separate posts — what makes each one read as one thing while scrolling, and what would break first at real feed size.

✎ Red-line notes mark what I'd change. Green marks what's already working.
01

Vitamin C Skincare

Product launch grid — citrus flatlay system
What works

The two-tone colour block — burnt orange plus warm cream — is the connective tissue holding together tiles shot in different styles (flatlay, hand-model, testimonial screenshot). It's doing more unifying work than the photography itself.

The bold "DID YOU KNOW" tile breaks the photo rhythm without breaking the palette, giving the eye a resting point mid-scroll.

What I'd change

The testimonial card and "NEW!" tile use a softer rounded-card style that reads as a different hand from the sharp colour blocks elsewhere — worth locking to one card treatment.

The same orange-citrus flatlay repeats across three tiles; at full-grid view it starts to feel like one photo cropped three ways rather than three shots.

Borrow this → a fixed two-tone colour block as the glue between photography that wasn't shot as a set.
02

Orélle Skincare

Radiance campaign grid — brand lockup system
What works

Same logo + tagline lockup, same corner, on every single tile — that alone makes nine visually different posts read as one campaign, even scrolling fast.

Chocolate brown instead of black as the "ink" colour is a smart, on-brand swap — warmer, ties directly to the skin tones in the photography.

What I'd change

Price tags ($65, $250) show up in a badge on one tile and as plain text on another — a small inconsistency that undercuts an otherwise tight system.

A few tiles carry a full paragraph of body copy — legible here, but that much text won't survive being compressed to feed-thumbnail size.

Borrow this → a persistent header lockup, same position on every tile, as the cheapest way to brand nine unrelated posts as one thing.
03

Lobello Marketing

Agency content grid — two-colour discipline
What works

Exactly two brand colours — blush pink and deep forest green — no third colour anywhere. The topics jump around (tips, testimonials, offers) but the palette never does.

The phone-mockup tile is a clever way to show social proof without a raw screenshot looking pasted into the grid.

What I'd change

The photo-to-colour-tile ratio leaves a stretch of three-plus solid-colour text tiles in a row in places — fine as a static grid preview, but that run would feel text-heavy scrolling a live feed.

Borrow this → the strict two-colour rule — pick exactly two brand colours and never introduce a third, even for the "special" tiles.
04

Personal Brand — "Good SMM"

Creator content grid — eyebrow-label system
What works

The repeated "@profilename / TOPIC" eyebrow in the same corner of every text tile turns nine one-off hooks into a recognisable, swipeable series.

Quiet editorial portraits alternating with plain-colour text tiles keep the grid from feeling like a slide deck.

What I'd change

The palette drifts between warm off-white, deep magenta, and a cooler blush across tiles — close enough to feel related, not close enough to feel locked.

Body copy size varies noticeably between tiles doing the same job (educational bullet lists), which reads as inconsistent hierarchy.

Borrow this → a one-line eyebrow label repeated in the same spot on every tile — turns a grid of unrelated hooks into a series.
05

Pink Lifestyle Moodboard

Aspirational grid — tone over subject
What works

The most disciplined palette of the five — every tile is a shade of pink plus black-and-white photography. Nothing competes with the mood.

Script, serif, and vertical-set type rotate across tiles, so a single-colour grid still feels varied rather than monotonous.

What I'd change

A few tiles have low type-to-background contrast — pale pink script on a slightly darker pink — that would get worse once compressed to a feed thumbnail.

The branded product placement is a real trademark risk to reuse directly in client work; swap for a generic or licensed prop.

Borrow this → proof a grid can cohere on tone alone — one colour family, disciplined type variation, no subject-matter thread needed.
—

Across All Five

”
A fixed anchor is what makes a grid read as one thing
Every strong grid here repeats something identical on every tile — a lockup, an eyebrow label, or a hard two-colour rule. That's the actual mechanism behind "cohesive grid," more than any single colour or font choice.
”
Tighter palettes read as more premium
Lobello and the pink moodboard, the two tightest palettes (one to two colours, zero drift), feel the most "designed." The SMM grid, which lets its pink drift across three related-but-different tones, feels the least locked-in by comparison.
”
Text-heavy tiles are the recurring weak point
Regardless of how strong the colour system is, the tiles carrying a full paragraph or list are the ones most likely to fail at real feed-thumbnail size — this shows up in Orélle, the SMM grid, and the pink moodboard alike.
