03 — Brand Strategy

What Let's Gooo stands for, before a single ad runs.

01

Adaptive, not generic

The plan changes because life does: a season ends, a trip gets booked, an injury happens.

02

Athlete-first, not appearance-first

Training is measured by performance and readiness, never by how someone looks.

03

Earned trust, not hype

Plain language, real and provable claims, no manufactured urgency.

04

One coach, every goal

A single relationship across every sport and season, not a new app per activity.

Voice

A knowledgeable peer, not a hype machine — plain-spoken, calm, collaborative. Not male-coded, in copy, casting, and imagery. The load-bearing word is "for" — always attached to a goal, never a person-type.

no "revolutionary" no "seamless" no "unlock your potential" no "beta/early user"

Competitive differentiation

The category includes single-sport training apps, generic workout content, and trackers that log without programming. Let's Gooo's claim is narrow and specific: one coach that reasons over a person's whole athletic year — not a piece of it. Every ad and post comes back to that.

04 — Product Launch Strategy

Marketing built around where the product actually is.

Let's Gooo is a paid subscription product that hasn't started charging yet — the plan has to move with that arc, not treat it as a generic app promotion.

Complete

Soft launch

Personal invites only — low dozens of users, all from the founders' own network.

Live now

Public launch

App Store listing is public — this plan is the app's first organic and paid acquisition, ever.

Mid-month

Monetization launch

Early-bird pricing banner goes live in-app; conversion-to-paid becomes measurable.

Month 2+

Full paid conversion

Standard pricing begins; retention and LTV join cost-per-signup as primary metrics.

Planning model — what to realistically expect

Grounded in 2026 Meta/Instagram benchmarks (CPM ~$12–18, CTR ~1–1.8%, iOS cost-per-install ~$4–8 for a niche lifestyle app), adjusted for a cold audience and zero prior data. Install→registration and registration→activation are working assumptions to replace with real numbers fast.

Applying a 40–55% activation rate on top, a realistic month lands somewhere between roughly 28 and 96 activated users. The point of week one isn't hitting a number in that range — it's narrowing the range for month two.

Kill criteria

If an angle's cost per signup runs 2–3x the other running tests after its first $50–75 of spend, stop it immediately and reallocate — don't wait out the week. A fast, cheap "no" is a good outcome for a test month.

08 — Reporting & What We Need From You

How we'll know it's working, and what's left to decide.

Weekly report

Best-performing posts, spend, CTR, CPC, cost per signup, winning creatives, and what to continue / stop / test next.

Day 15 checkpoint

Which angles are trending toward a kill, which toward a winner, and whether attribution is holding up under real spend.

Week 4 readout

A verdict per belief, a per-platform readout, funnel economics, and a specific month-two recommendation.

Decisions we need from you

App Store listing (ASO): keywords, screenshots, and description affect every paid click's install rate.
Referral / invite: is there a shareable "invite a friend" link worth building content around?
Post-signup nurture: what pushes a new user toward their first logged workout?
Early price timing: build creative around it now, or hold until it's live?
Benchmarks feature: confirm as soon as it's safe to reference publicly.
Claim guardrails: anything creating friction worth a gut check?
How founder time is used

One working session, Day 1 — everything else runs solo from there.

Occasional quick yes/no confirmations when content touches something not yet locked.

An optional 10–15 minute weekly check-in, if useful — not a standing requirement.
