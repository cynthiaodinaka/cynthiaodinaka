Hydra Glow

A single hero ad for GLAMFOX's Rosehip Oil & Hyaluron face cream — built to feel like a fragrance campaign rather than a skincare listing.

01

The Brief

GLAMFOX needed the jar to look like a prize, not a product photo. Rosehip oil and hyaluronic acid are common ingredients on a crowded shelf — the ad had to earn "premium" through presentation alone, since the formula story is one most competitors can also tell.

The brief leaned toward a beauty-editorial mood: a pedestal moment, not a catalogue shot, that would still carry the SKU details (50g, ingredients, site URL) needed for a paid placement.

  • AMake a small 50g jar feel like the hero of a full campaign, not a cropped product photo
  • BSignal "rosehip + hyaluron" through mood and colour, not just an ingredient list
  • CFit brand name, weight, formula, and URL in without breaking the editorial feel

Design notes / 4
1
Serif wordmark sets the register
"GLAMFOX" runs in a high-contrast display serif rather than a beauty-standard sans — that one choice is what pushes the ad from "skincare" toward "fragrance campaign."
2
Halo ring frames the product
A glowing ring sits behind the jar like a spotlight — it gives the eye a single place to land before it ever reaches the label copy.
3
Podium + glass disc build a stage
The mirrored disc under the jar does two jobs at once: it reads as a reflection (premium, editorial) and as a plinth (the jar is a prize being presented).
4
Rib lines add texture without noise
Fine vertical stripes on the right edge keep the flat pink background from feeling empty, echoing fluted glass packaging without competing with the jar.

Weight, formula subhead, and site URL placed top and bottom, then the whole canvas zoomed out to check the full composition.

05

Why It Works

Editorial, not catalogue
The podium-and-halo staging borrows from fragrance advertising, not skincare listings — it's the fastest way to make a 50g jar feel expensive.
One hue, full commitment
Every element — ring, podium, glass, background — stays inside the same pink family, so the ad reads as a considered set rather than a stock backdrop.
Built in layers, checked at scale
Staging, background, and copy were added as separate passes and the canvas zoomed out at the end — catching legibility issues before export, not after.
