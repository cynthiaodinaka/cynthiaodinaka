Case Study — 002

Cream Ritual

Two Instagram feed ads for a Dove hand-cream range — built to stop the scroll with a single oversized word, then close the sale with the ingredient shot underneath it.

Client
Dove — Hand Cream Range
Deliverable
2× Instagram feed ad tiles
Platform

Studio
The PixBow, Dhaka
01

The Brief

The client needed feed ads that read in under a second. Hand-cream ads live between recipe videos and outfit posts — most people are scrolling, not shopping. The job was to make the product legible instantly, communicate "for dry skin" and the hero ingredient without forcing anyone to read a paragraph, and still feel premium enough for a Dove shelf.

Two variants were needed — Avocado & Calendula and Coconut & Almond Milk — sharing one system so they'd read as a matching pair in-feed, but each keeping its own ingredient personality.

  • ASell "for dry skin" in a glance, no headline required to get the message
  • BKeep the pack legible at thumbnail size — it's still a product ad, not just a mood shot
  • CBuild one template that flexes across two very different ingredient palettes

Design notes / 4
1
Pack kept upright and central
The tube is tilted for energy but never rotated past the point where the "Dove" logo stops reading — thumbnail legibility beats dynamism every time.
2
Headline in the reader's language
"Питание сухой кожи" sits top-left, the natural first-read position, so the benefit lands before the eye ever reaches the pack.
3
Oversized wordmark as background texture
"КРЕМ" runs full-bleed behind the product at a size no one needs to read — it's there to give the flat background weight and a sense of scale before you consciously register it as a word.
4
Ingredient proof, numbered like a spec sheet
Avocado and calendula are called out with the same [1] / [2] index device used on-pack — borrowing the brand's own visual language instead of inventing a new one.

Exhibit — Coconut

Body Love / coconut variant. Same system, warmer palette — built to sit next to the avocado tile without competing against it.

Design notes / 4
1
Milk splash carries the motion
Where the avocado tile gets its energy from the tilted tube, this one gets it from the almond-milk splash — a softer, more indulgent kind of movement to match "Restoring Care."
2
Warm palette swap, same grid
Gold foil and toasted-almond tones replace the avocado green, but the headline block, wordmark, and ingredient index sit on the exact same coordinates — that's what makes the pair feel like one campaign.
3
Cracked coconut anchors the base
A whole, cracked coconut replaces the halved avocado — the "cut open to show what's inside" shot is the one piece of visual grammar kept identical across both ads.
4
Volume badge as trust mark
The circular "75 ml" stamp with its certification microtype does double duty — it reads as a seal of authenticity at a glance, and as real product info on closer look.

05

Process

Step 1
Unpack the brief
Pulled the two hero benefits — "for dry skin," ingredient story — and ranked them: pack ID first, benefit second, ingredients third.
Step 2
Build the skeleton
Locked headline zone, wordmark scale, product placement, and index position on a grid before touching either variant's artwork.
Step 3
Dress each variant
Sourced ingredient art per SKU, matched the accent palette, and set the tilt/composition of the pack and produce shot.
Step 4
Stress-test at thumbnail size
Shrunk both tiles to feed size to confirm the logo, headline, and volume badge still read before shipping.
06

Why It Works

Reads before it's read
The oversized wordmark and tilted pack communicate "cream, premium, for you" before anyone processes a single word — critical for a scroll-speed medium.
One system, two products
Because the grid is fixed, this template scales to the rest of the range without a redesign — only the accent colour and ingredient art need to change.
Borrows the brand's own grammar
The numbered ingredient index isn't an invented device — it's lifted straight from Dove's own packaging language, so the ad feels like an extension of the product, not an ad bolted onto it.
